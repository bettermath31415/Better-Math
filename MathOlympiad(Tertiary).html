Question 1

Determine the limit \(\displaystyle \lim_{x\to 0} \frac{1}{x} e^{-1/x^2}\).

Show Solution

Substitute \(y = 1/x\), so as \(x\to 0\), \(y \to \infty\) (from the appropriate side): \[ \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{x}e^{-1/x^2} = \lim_{y\to\infty} y e^{-y^2} = \lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{y}{e^{y^2}}. \] By L'Hospital's rule, \(\displaystyle \lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{y}{e^{y^2}} = \lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{1}{2ye^{y^2}} = 0.\)

Answer: 0

Question 2

Max lies and tells the truth alternately when asked about his favourite number (his first answer may be either). Bob asks:

  • Is your number less than 100?
  • Is it a prime?
  • Does it contain the digit 4?
  • Is it even?
  • Is it a square?

His answer is always "yes". What is Max's favourite number?

Show Solution

If the first answer were a lie, the 2nd and 4th (also lies/truths alternating) would force the number to be an even prime, i.e. 2 — but then the first statement would have been true, a contradiction. So the first answer is the truth: the number is less than 100. Then odd-indexed answers are true, even-indexed are lies: it is a square (true 3rd), not even i.e. odd (lie 4th), and contains a 4 (true... wait apply pattern) — working through the alternation: number \(<100\) (true), not prime (lie means it isn't prime — consistent since it's a square \(>1\)), contains a 4 (true), not even i.e. odd (lie on "is it even"), and is a square (true). Among squares under 100 (1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81), the only odd one containing digit 4 is 49.

Answer: 49

Question 3

A rectangular box has three face diagonals of length \(\sqrt{34}\), \(\sqrt{58}\), \(\sqrt{74}\). Find the volume.

Show Solution

Let sides be \(a,b,c\). Then \(a^2+b^2=34,\ a^2+c^2=58,\ b^2+c^2=74\). Adding the first two and subtracting the third: \(2a^2 = 34+58-74=18\), so \(a=3\), and then \(b=5,\ c=7\).

Answer: Volume \(=3\cdot5\cdot7=105\)

Question 6

Six leaders sit in a row for a photo. Helen and Patricia must sit together; Jacob and Julius refuse to sit together; Tokyo and Trevor don't mind. How many seatings?

Show Solution

Treat Helen+Patricia as one block: \(5!=120\) arrangements, times 2 orderings of the pair \(= 240\). Subtract arrangements where Jacob and Julius also sit together: treat them as a block too, giving \(4!=24\) arrangements, times \(2\times2=4\) for the two pairs' internal orders \(=96\).

Answer: \(2\cdot5! - 2\cdot2\cdot4! = 240-96=144\)

Question 7

Find \(f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) with \(f(0)=1\), such that \(\int_{-\infty}^x f(t)\,dt\) and \(\int_{-\infty}^x f(t)^2\,dt\) both converge and \[ \left(\int_{-\infty}^x f(t)\,dt\right)^2 = \int_{-\infty}^x f(t)^2\,dt \quad \text{for all }x. \]

Show Solution

Let \(F(x)=\int_{-\infty}^x f(t)\,dt\). Differentiating both sides: \(2F(x)f(x) = f(x)^2\), so \(f(x)=0\) or \(2F(x)=f(x)=F'(x)\). The differential equation \(F'=2F\) gives \(F(x)=Ce^{2x}\), so \(f(x)=2Ce^{2x}\). Using \(f(0)=1\): \(C=\frac12\).

Answer: \(f(x)=e^{2x}\)

Question 8

A clock's minute hand has length 4, hour hand length 3. Find the distance between the tips when this distance is increasing most rapidly.

Show Solution

By the law of cosines, \(x^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C\) where \(C\) is the angle between the hands (changing at constant rate), \(a=4,b=3\). Differentiating implicitly and using the law of sines gives \(\frac{dx}{dC}=b\sin A\), maximised when \(A=90^\circ\). Then by Pythagoras \(x=\sqrt{a^2-b^2}=\sqrt7\).

Answer: \(\sqrt7\)

Question 19

Show Solution
