Problem 1

Given that $$\frac{1+3+5+\cdots+(2n-1)}{2+4+6+\cdots+(2n)}=\frac{2011}{2012},$$ determine $n$.

Show Solution
Sum each arithmetic progression as (number of terms) $\times$ (average of first, last term): $$1+3+\cdots+(2n-1)=n\cdot\frac{1+(2n-1)}{2}=n^2,\qquad 2+4+\cdots+2n=n\cdot\frac{2+2n}{2}=n(n+1).$$ So the equation becomes $$\frac{n^2}{n(n+1)}=\frac{n}{n+1}=\frac{2011}{2012}.$$ Since consecutive integers are always coprime, both $\frac{n}{n+1}$ and $\frac{2011}{2012}$ are already in lowest terms, so matching numerators and denominators gives $n=2011$.

Problem 2

Let $ABCD$ be a square and $X$ a point such that $A$ and $X$ are on opposite sides of $CD$. The lines $AX$ and $BX$ intersect $CD$ in $Y$ and $Z$ respectively. If the area of $ABCD$ is $1$ and the area of $XYZ$ is $\tfrac23$, determine the length of $YZ$.

Show Solution
Since $CD\parallel AB$, we have $\triangle XYZ\sim\triangle XAB$. Let $YZ=x$ and let $y$ be the height from $X$ to line $CD$; the height from $X$ to $AB$ is then $y+1$. Similarity gives $$\frac{x}{1}=\frac{y}{y+1}.$$ Area of $\triangle XYZ$: $$\text{area}(XYZ)=\frac12 xy=\frac12\cdot\frac{y}{y+1}\cdot y=\frac{y^2}{2(y+1)}=\frac23.$$ $$\Rightarrow 3y^2=4(y+1)\ \Rightarrow\ 3y^2-4y-4=0\ \Rightarrow\ (y-2)(3y+2)=0.$$ Since $y>0$, $y=2$, so $$x=\frac{y}{y+1}=\frac{2}{3}.$$

Problem 3

Sixty points on a circle are coloured: thirty red, twenty blue, ten green, dividing the circle into sixty arcs. An arc between a red and green point is assigned $1$; between red and blue, $2$; between blue and green, $3$; between two points of the same colour, $0$. What is the greatest possible sum of all the numbers assigned to the arcs?

Show Solution
Score the points: red $=0$, green $=1$, blue $=2$. Check each arc type matches the sum of endpoint scores: $$\text{red–green: }0+1=1,\quad \text{red–blue: }0+2=2,\quad \text{blue–green: }1+2=3,$$ and same-colour arcs score $0 \le$ sum of endpoint scores. So every arc's number $\le$ sum of its endpoints' scores. Each point is an endpoint of 2 arcs, so $$\text{total} \le 2\sum_{\text{points}}\text{score} = 2(30\cdot0+20\cdot2+10\cdot1)=100.$$ Equality requires avoiding green–green and blue–blue arcs (their score $0<$ sum of endpoint scores). This is achievable: alternate red with non-red around the circle, and arrange the 20 blue/10 green among the non-red slots so no two greens are adjacent (possible since blue outnumbers green). So the greatest possible sum is $\boxed{100}$.

Problem 5

Let $ABC$ be a triangle with $AB\ne AC$, orthocentre $H$, circumcentre $O$, and $D$ the midpoint of $BC$. The extensions of $HD$ and $AO$ meet at $P$. Prove that $\triangle AHP$ and $\triangle ABC$ have the same centroid.

Show Solution
Let $X$ be the second intersection of line $AO$ with the circumcircle, so $AX$ is a diameter. By Thales' theorem, $\angle ABX=90^\circ$, so $BX\perp AB$; since $CH\perp AB$ too, $BX\parallel CH$. Likewise $CX\parallel BH$. So $BHCX$ has both pairs of opposite sides parallel — it's a parallelogram, whose diagonals $BC$ and $HX$ bisect each other. Their common midpoint is $D$. So $X$ lies on $AO$ (by construction) and on $HD$ (since $D$ is the midpoint of $HX$) — hence $X=P$, and $D$ is the midpoint of $HP$. Thus $AD$ is a median of both $\triangle ABC$ (to side $BC$) and $\triangle AHP$ (to side $HP$). Since the centroid always sits $\frac23$ of the way along any median from the vertex, both triangles share the same centroid, at the point $\frac23$ of the way from $A$ to $D$ on $AD$.

Problem 6

Find all functions $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $$f(km)+f(kn)-f(k)f(mn)\ge 1$$ for all $k,m,n\in\mathbb{N}$.

Show Solution
$k=m=n=1$: $2f(1)-f(1)^2\ge1 \Rightarrow (f(1)-1)^2\le0 \Rightarrow f(1)=1$. $k=1,m=n$: $2f(n)-f(n^2)\ge1$. $\quad$ $k=n,m=1$: $f(n^2)+f(n)-f(n)^2\ge1$. Adding (the $f(n^2)$ terms cancel): $$3f(n)-f(n)^2\ge2 \ \Rightarrow\ (f(n)-1)(f(n)-2)\le0 \ \Rightarrow\ 1\le f(n)\le2 \text{ for all }n.$$ Suppose $f(n)=a$ with $11$ (since $a>1$): $$\frac{1-a+a^2}{a}=1+\frac{(a-1)^2}{a}=b \ \Rightarrow\ f(n^2)\ge b\cdot f(n).$$ Iterating (the same bound applies at $n^2, n^4,\dots$ since each is still $>1$): $$f\big(n^{2^j}\big)\ge b^j f(n)\xrightarrow{j\to\infty}\infty,$$ contradicting $f\le2$. So $f(n)\le1$ for all $n$; combined with $f(n)\ge1$, $$f(n)\equiv 1,$$ which indeed satisfies the inequality: $f(km)+f(kn)-f(k)f(mn)=1+1-1=1\ge1$.

Problem 1

2013 is the first year since the Middle Ages consisting of four consecutive digits. How many such years are there still to come after 2013 (and before the year 10000)?

Show Solution
There are $7$ choices of four consecutive digits ($0,1,2,3$ up to $6,7,8,9$), each giving $4!=24$ arrangements: $7\times24=168$ possibilities. Subtract those starting with $0$ ($6$: from $0,1,2,3$), those starting with $1$ ($12$: from $1,0,2,3$ or $1,2,3,4$), and $2013$ itself: $168-19=\boxed{149}$.

Problem 4

Determine all pairs of polynomials $f,g$ with real coefficients satisfying $x^2\cdot g(x)=f(g(x))$.

Show Solution
Write $f(x)=c_0+c_1x+\cdots+c_nx^n$. Then $x^2g(x)=c_0+c_1g(x)+\cdots+c_ng(x)^n$. The left side and every term but $c_0$ on the right are divisible by $g(x)$, so $c_0=0$ unless $g$ is constant. If $g\equiv K$ constant: $Kx^2=f(K)$ forces $K=0$, $f$ any polynomial with $f(0)=0$. If $g$ non-constant, $c_0=0$: dividing by $g(x)$, $x^2=c_1+c_2g(x)+\cdots+c_ng(x)^{n-1}$. With $\deg g=m$, the right side has degree $m(n-1)=2$, giving $(m,n)=(1,3)$ or $(2,2)$:
  • $g(x)=ax+b$, $f(x)=\dfrac{x(x-b)^2}{a^2}$ (any $a\ne0,b$);
  • $g(x)=\dfrac{x^2-c_1}{c_2}$, $f(x)=c_1x+c_2x^2$ ($c_2\ne0$, $c_1$ arbitrary).
These, together with the $g\equiv0$ family, are all solutions.

Problem 5

Coins are placed on a $20\times13$ board. Two coins are neighbours if they share a row or column with no coin between them. Find the largest number of coins so that no coin has more than two neighbours.

Show Solution
On an $m\times n$ board ($m,n>1$) the maximum is $m+n$. Achieved by placing coins along the top row, the rightmost column, and the bottom-left square. Upper bound, by induction on $m+n$: suppose $m\le n$ and $m+n+1$ coins are placed. By pigeonhole, some row has $\ge\lceil(m+n+1)/m\rceil\ge3$ coins, so a middle one already has 2 neighbours in its row and must be alone in its column. Deleting that column leaves an $m\times(n-1)$ board with $m+n$ coins, contradicting the inductive hypothesis. For $20\times13$: $20+13=\boxed{33}$.

Problem 1

Determine the last two digits of the product of the squares of all positive odd integers less than $2014$.

Show Solution
The product $P$ of all odd integers less than $2014$ includes $25$ as a factor and is odd, so its last two digits are $25$ or $75$; write $P=100n+25$ or $100n+75$. Problem 2

Given $\dfrac{a-b}{c-d}=2$ and $\dfrac{a-c}{b-d}=3$, determine $\dfrac{a-d}{b-c}$.

Show Solution
Let $x=c-d$, $y=b-d$. Then $a-d=(c-d)+(a-c)=x+3y$ and $a-d=(b-d)+(a-b)=y+2x$, so $x+3y=y+2x\Rightarrow x=2y$. Then $a-d=5y$ and $b-c=(b-d)-(c-d)=y-x=-y$, so $$\frac{a-d}{b-c}=\boxed{-5}.$$

Problem 5

Let $n>1$. An $n\times n$ grid has $n$ green and $n$ blue unit squares (rest white). Are there more colourings with no two green in a row and no two blue in a column, or colourings with no two green and no two blue in the same row?

Show Solution
If $n$ green squares occupy distinct rows, each row has $n-1$ remaining squares, giving $(n-1)^n$ ways to place $n$ non-repeating-row blue squares. For the other count, if $x_i$ is the number of non-green squares in column $i$ ($\sum x_i=n^2-n$), the number of column-safe blue placements is $x_1x_2\cdots x_n\le\left(\frac{n^2-n}{n}\right)^n=(n-1)^n$ by AM–GM, with strict inequality possible. So there are (weakly) more colourings of the first type (no two green, no two blue in the same row) than of the second type.

Taking $m=22^1\cdot23^{20}$ and $n=22^2\cdot23^{19}$ (two different positive integers), we get $f(m)=f(n)$, completing the proof.
