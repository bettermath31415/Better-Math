South African Mathematical Olympiad(SAMO) - Third Round

Senior Division (Grades 10–12)

The South African Mathematics Olympiad (SAMO) is a prestigious national competition that challenges and inspires learners to develop advanced mathematical knowledge, critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Open to learners in Grades 8 to 12, the competition takes place over three increasingly challenging rounds, with top performers gaining recognition, awards, and opportunities to represent South Africa at international competitions such as the Pan African Mathematics Olympiad and the International Mathematical Olympiad.

Problem 1

The value of $\dfrac14+0.025$ is

(A) 0.05   (B) $\dfrac12$   (C) 0.375   (D) $\dfrac38$   (E) $\dfrac{11}{40}$

Show Solution

Answer: E.

Problem 7

Problem 8

If your watch loses 5 minutes each hour and you set it correctly at 07:00, what is the actual time when your watch shows 09:45?

(A) 09:55   (B) 10:00   (C) 10:05   (D) 10:10   (E) 10:15

Show Solution

Answer: B.

The watch gains 55 real minutes per 60 shown minutes... actually loses: for every true hour, the watch advances only 55 minutes. From 07:00 to 09:45 on the watch is 165 = $3\times55$ minutes, so true time elapsed is $3\times60=180$ minutes, i.e. 3 hours. True time is 10:00.

Problem 6

Let $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N_0$ satisfy $f(mn)=mf(n)+nf(m)$ for all $m,n$, and $f(2024)=10120$. Prove there exist $m\ne n$ with $f(m)=f(n)$.

Show Solution
$f(1)=0$; if $f(m)=0$ for some $m\ge2$ we are done ($f(m)=f(1)$), so assume $f(m)\ge1$ for all $m\ge2$. Induction on the functional equation gives $f(a^n)=na^{n-1}f(a)$ for any $a$ (base cases $f(a^2)=2af(a)$, $f(a^3)=3a^2f(a)$ check directly). Since $2024=2^3\cdot11\cdot23$, applying the equation twice, $$f(2024)=f(8\cdot11\cdot23)=23f(8\cdot11)+88f(23)=23\big(11f(8)+8f(11)\big)+88f(23)=253f(8)+184f(11)+88f(23),$$ and $f(8)=f(2^3)=3\cdot2^2\cdot f(2)=12f(2)$, so $$10120=f(2024)=253\cdot12\,f(2)+184f(11)+88f(23)=3036f(2)+184f(11)+88f(23).\tag{$\dagger$}$$ Working modulo $11$, $23$, and $8$ constrains the possible values of $f(11)$, $f(23)$, $f(2)$ respectively; combined with $f(2)\ge2,\ f(11)\ge11,\ f(23)\ge23$ (from $f(a)\ge1$ applied via the multiplicative rule), these minimal values are exactly the ones satisfying $(\dagger)$, forcing equality: $$f(2)=2,\quad f(11)=11,\quad f(23)=23.$$ Since $2\cdot11=22$ is close to $23$, consider $m=22^a23^c$. Using $f(22)=2f(11)+11f(2)=2(11)+11(2)=44$ and the power formula $f(a^n)=na^{n-1}f(a)$: $$f(22^a23^c)=23^cf(22^a)+22^af(23^c)=23^c(a\cdot22^{a-1}\cdot44)+22^a(c\cdot23^{c-1}\cdot23)=(2a+c)\cdot22^a23^c.$$ Now compare $f(22^a23^{b+1})=(2a+b+1)\,22^a23^{b+1}$ with $f(22^{a+1}23^b)=(2(a+1)+b)\,22^{a+1}23^b$. These two expressions are equal whenever $$2a+b+1=22\quad\text{and}\quad2a+b+2=23$$ hold simultaneously — and indeed both reduce to the single condition $2a+b=21$, satisfied by e.g. $(a,b)=(1,19)$. Taking $m=22^1\cdot23^{20}$ and $n=22^2\cdot23^{19}$ (two different positive integers), we get $f(m)=f(n)$, completing the proof.
